1 · Model details

                        
ONE-HealthRecord is a composite One Health EHR system with five components: a deterministic rule extractor (audit reference), MedGemma 4B (primary clinical extractor), companion-animal and production-animal LoRA adapters, and a gradient-boosted Risk Model. The pipeline emits FHIR R4 with full Provenance on every machine-authored field.

                        
Card author: ONE-HealthRecord project team · License: Apache 2.0 (project code); MedGemma weights under HAI Developer Foundations terms.

                    

                    

                        
2 · Intended use

                        
Primary uses: structured FHIR generation from clinical & veterinary dictation; cross-species cluster surfacing for public-health surveillance; risk-triaged contact tracing; equity-aware analytics.

                        
Primary users: clinicians, veterinarians (Clinical Workspace); state and tribal public-health authorities, ADHS, USDA APHIS (Public Health Console).

                        
Out of scope: direct-to-consumer triage; insurance-denial decisions; replacement of clinical or veterinary judgment; individual-level public-health enforcement; military or law-enforcement use.

                    

                    

                        
3 · Factors

                        
Performance is evaluated against: species (human vs animal, then per species class), county type (urban / rural / tribal), language (English at MVP; Spanish / Diné planned), age band, household size, sex, care setting (primary care / urgent care / vet GP / vet emergency).

8 · Privacy & security architecture

                

                    Four-layer privacy stack. Each layer addresses a distinct attack surface; the layers are stacked, not chosen.
                

                

                    

                        
L4

                        

                            
Output privacy — Laplace mechanism on aggregate counts

                            
ε = 1.0, sensitivity 1. Already shipped. Active on the public-facing surveillance feed (Sentinel Alerts tab). Defends against re-identification via published counts.

                        

                    

                    

                        
L3

                        

                            
Secure aggregation — Bonawitz et al. (CCS 2017)

                            
Additive secret-sharing on weight exchange. Only the sum of all sites' updates is reconstructible, and only after a quorum contributes. Defends against reconstruction of any individual site's update.

                        

                    

                    

                        
L2

                        

                            
DP-SGD inside each site's local training step — Opacus

                            
Per-example gradient clipping (clip = 1.0) + Gaussian noise (σ = 1.1) on each minibatch. Target privacy budget (ε ≤ 8.0, δ = 1e-5). Defends against training-data extraction via model inversion.

                        

                    

                    

                        
L1

                        

                            
Swarm learning topology — no central aggregator

                            
Peer-to-peer training across hospital, vet, ADHS, tribal-health, and APHIS sites. Coordinated by a permissioned blockchain ledger. Defends against aggregator compromise and aggregator coercion. Federated learning is documented as fallback.

                        

                    

                


                
Federated-learning simulation results

                

                    Six simulated sites, FedAvg aggregation, 10 rounds, real metrics. Comparable AUC to centralized training validates the approach.
                

                

                

            


            
            

                
9 · Ethical considerations

                

                    

                        
Dual-use risk: biosurveillance overreach

                        
A One Health surveillance system could be misused to surveil rather than serve. Mitigation: role-based access, audit logs in Provenance.entity, action-severity alerts require human review before any non-medical action; published counts are Laplace-noised so individual-level inference is bounded.

                    

                    

                        
Tribal data sovereignty (CARE principles)

                        
Apache and Navajo households appear in this dataset. The 8.6-pp tribal-land AUC gap is documented as the headline equity finding. Production deployment requires consultation with tribal IRBs and adherence to the CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics).

                    

                    

                        
Equity in alerting

                        
Per the disaggregation in the Risk Model tab, alert sensitivity differs across rural / urban / tribal slices. Action items: stratified resampling in the next training round; explicit reporting of per-stratum recall in production dashboards; community advisory board for alert thresholds.

                    

                    

                        
Veterinary client confidentiality

                        
Vet records are property of the veterinary client, not the animal. Cross-species linkage requires dual consent — patient and animal-owner — at intake. Workflow design in docs/medgemma_integration.md §6.

                    

                

            


            
            

                
10 · Caveats & recommendations

                

                    Honest scope: The MVP is a synthetic-data demonstration. The privacy architecture is implemented and verifiable on a single laptop (federated-simulation runs end-to-end); the production deployment topology is documented and ready but requires partner organizations and partner data.
                

                

                    

                        
What we cannot do today

                        
    
                            
  • Real-data evaluation (i2b2, n2c2, MIMIC-IV, VetCompass) — needs credentialed access
    • 
                            
  • Production-scale swarm learning across organizations — needs partner sites
    • 
                            
  • Live MedGemma inference on vet dictations — needs API key & species LoRA training
    • 
                            
  • Spanish or Diné language extraction — needs corpus & community review
    • 
                            
  • Audio (Whisper ASR) — text dictation only at MVP
    • 
                        

                    

                    

                        
What downstream users should NOT infer

                        
    
                            
  • The risk score is a decision-support probability, not a clinical diagnosis
    • 
                            
  • An alert firing is not by itself sufficient for any non-medical action
    • 
                            
  • Synthetic-data F1 does not predict performance on real clinical text
    • 
                            
  • DP-SGD ε ≤ 8.0 is a training-run guarantee, not a per-prediction guarantee
    • 
                            
  • The blockchain ledger reveals participation timing (side channel)
    •

Federation · per-site FHIR shadow stores · live wire log

                

                    ONE-HealthRecord is not the system of record. Each partner site runs its own FHIR server populated from its native EHR (Epic, Cerner, IDEXX VetFHIR, Covetrus). The application acts as a federated consumer — every patient view fans out as cross-site FHIR queries that you can watch fire in the wire log below. No primary clinical record is held centrally.

Audit log · session events for the current browser

                

                    Every login, sign-out, hub change, tab navigation, and access-denial event is recorded in localStorage with timestamp, user_id, role, action, and target. In production this stream feeds the FHIR Provenance.entity resource and the SOC audit pipeline. Showing the audit surface to the user is itself an accountability control — they can see what the system has recorded about their own session.
